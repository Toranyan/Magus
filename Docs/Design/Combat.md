# Combat

## Overview

Combat is a battle of magical understanding.

Victory comes from recognizing threats, understanding elemental relationships, and selecting the appropriate response.

The player is not expected to memorize rigid solutions.

Instead, the player is encouraged to understand how elements, statuses, enemies, and spells interact.

Combat should reward knowledge and creativity.

---

# Core Combat Loop

1. Observe the battlefield.
2. Identify the most important threat or opportunity.
3. Select an appropriate spell.
4. Create or exploit an interaction.
5. Gain an advantage.
6. Adapt to the new combat state.

Combat is a continuous cycle of observation and response.

Every spell cast should meaningfully change the battlefield.

---

# Prepared Spells

The player can prepare three spells.

Prepared spells can be cast immediately.

The prepared spell loadout represents the player's current strategy.

Changing prepared spells is a meaningful decision.

Players should regularly consider:

* What threats am I facing?
* What interactions am I need access to?
* What element am I lacking?

Preparation is a form of mastery.

---

# Countering

Countering is a core mechanic.

Many attacks, abilities, statuses, and enemies can be countered.

A successful counter should create a stronger outcome than merely avoiding the threat.

Example:

Enemy launches a Fire attack.

Possible responses:

* Dodge
* Block
* Counter with Water

All responses are valid.

The counter response should create the greatest advantage.

Examples:

* Extinguishing the attack
* Creating a vulnerable state
* Reflecting the attack
* Generating a beneficial interaction

Countering should feel powerful.

Countering should never feel mandatory.

---

# Elemental Opposition

Elements exist in opposing pairs.

* Fire ↔ Water
* Earth ↔ Wind
* Light ↔ Dark

Opposing elements naturally disrupt one another.

The purpose of elemental opposition is to create opportunities rather than hard weaknesses.

The player should think:

"What interaction can I create?"

rather than:

"What is this enemy weak to?"

---

# Status Effects

Status effects are one of the primary interaction systems.

Examples:

* Burning
* Wet
* Frozen
* Charged
* Corrupted
* Revealed

Statuses should:

* Influence decision making
* Create opportunities
* Enable combinations
* Encourage experimentation

Most powerful outcomes should emerge from combining effects.

---

# Spell Philosophy

Every spell should support at least one of the following:

* Offense
* Defense
* Support

Ideally, every spell contributes to multiple categories.

Examples:

A Fire spell may deal damage while also creating area denial.

A Water spell may defend while creating future combo opportunities.

A Wind spell may reposition enemies while enabling offense.

Avoid creating spells that have only one purpose.

---

# Combat Roles

Each element has a natural tendency.

## Fire

Primary Tendency: Offense

Creates pressure, damage, and battlefield threats.

## Water

Primary Tendency: Support

Controls situations and creates opportunities.

## Wind

Primary Tendency: Support

Manipulates positioning and movement.

## Earth

Primary Tendency: Defense

Provides protection and stability.

## Light

Primary Tendency: Defense

Provides protection, awareness, and purification.

## Dark

Primary Tendency: Offense

Exploits weaknesses and manipulates resources.

These tendencies guide player expectations but do not restrict spell design.

---

# Combat Goals

The player should constantly be asking:

* What is the biggest threat?
* What opportunity exists right now?
* Which element gives me the greatest advantage?
* Can I create a stronger interaction?
* Should I counter or conserve resources?

Combat should reward understanding.

Combat should reward adaptation.

Combat should reward creativity.

---

# Enemy Design Principles

Enemies should teach the combat system.

Every enemy should encourage one or more of the following:

* Countering
* Positioning
* Status interactions
* Elemental interactions
* Resource management

Enemies should create interesting decisions rather than demanding specific answers.

Avoid enemies that can only be defeated by a single spell.

---

# Success Conditions

Combat is successful when:

* Multiple solutions exist.
* Different spell loadouts remain viable.
* Players discover interactions naturally.
* Countering feels rewarding.
* Knowledge is more important than memorization.
* Players feel clever.

The player should win because they understood the situation, not because they executed a predetermined sequence.
